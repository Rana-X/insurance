Cyber Protection Policy

for Small and Mid-Sized Businesses  ·  Form HIC-CY-100 (10/26)

This policy is a contract between you and us. In return for your premium, and relying on the statements in your Application, we agree to provide the coverage described below, subject to the Declarations and all the terms of this policy.

Throughout this policy, “you” and “your” mean the Named Insured and, where the context requires, every other Insured. “We,” “us” and “our” mean Harborline Insurance Company. Other words in bold have the meanings given in Section II. The coverage letters A through R are the same as in Item 6 of the Declarations.

Section I · What We Cover

Section IWhat We Cover

How coverage is triggered

1.First-party coverages (Coverages A–H, M, N, O, P and R) apply to an Incident that you first Discover during the Policy Period and report to us as Section V.A requires.

2.Liability coverages (Coverages I–L and Q) apply to a Claim first made against an Insured during the Policy Period or any Extended Reporting Period, and reported to us as Section V.A requires, for a Wrongful Act committed on or after the Retroactive Date.

3.Every payment is subject to the limits, Retention, Waiting Period and coinsurance terms in Section IV and the Declarations.

4.Optional coverages (Part 2) apply only if Item 6 of the Declarations shows them as purchased.

Part 1 — Core coverages

AIncident Response Services

In shortOne call to our hotline gets you a breach lawyer and a forensic team. The first 72 hours cost you nothing and do not reduce your limit.

If you report an actual or suspected Incident to our Hotline during the Policy Period, we will provide Incident Response Services through our Panel Providers for the first 72 hours after your report. We will provide these services even if it later turns out that no Incident occurred.

Incident Response Services are provided in addition to the Policy Aggregate Limit, and no Retention applies. Services after the first 72 hours are covered under the coverage that applies to them (for example, Coverage B, C or F), subject to that coverage's limit and the Retention.

BBreach Response Costs

In shortWe pay to investigate a breach, meet your legal duties to notify people, and protect the people affected.

We will pay Breach Response Costs that you incur because of an actual or reasonably suspected Security Failure or Privacy Event that you first Discover during the Policy Period.

CCyber Extortion and Ransomware

In shortIf criminals threaten your systems or data and demand payment, we pay experts to respond and, where the law allows, the payment itself.

We will pay Extortion Costs that you incur, with our prior written consent, to respond to a Cyber Extortion Threat that you first Discover during the Policy Period. Section V.F sets out the steps that apply before any Extortion Payment is made.

DBusiness Interruption

In shortIf a cyberattack takes your systems down for more than 8 hours, we pay the income you lose and the extra costs of keeping the business running, for up to 180 days.

We will pay Business Interruption Loss that you incur during the Period of Restoration as a direct result of an Interruption of your Computer System caused by a Security Failure that you first Discover during the Policy Period.

We pay only if the Interruption lasts longer than the Waiting Period, and only for Business Interruption Loss incurred after the Waiting Period ends.

EDependent Business Interruption

In shortThe same protection as Coverage D when the attack hits an IT or cloud provider you rely on, rather than your own systems.

We will pay Business Interruption Loss that you incur during the Period of Restoration as a direct result of an Interruption of a Dependent Business's computer systems caused by a Security Failure of those systems that you first Discover during the Policy Period.

We pay only if the Interruption lasts longer than the Waiting Period, and only for Business Interruption Loss incurred after the Waiting Period ends.

FData and System Restoration

In shortWe pay to get your data and software back to the way they were before an attack, and to remove malware.

We will pay Restoration Costs that you incur because of a Security Failure that you first Discover during the Policy Period.

GSecurity Improvement Costs

In shortAfter a covered attack, we help pay for the fixes our experts recommend so it does not happen again.

If we pay Loss under Coverage B, C, D or F because of an Incident, we will also pay Security Improvement Costs that you incur within 90 days after that Incident is contained, up to the limit shown for Coverage G in Item 6 of the Declarations. No separate Retention applies to this coverage.

HSocial Engineering and Funds Transfer Fraud

In shortIf you are tricked into sending money to a criminal, or a criminal breaks into your accounts and moves money, we reimburse you. Verifying payment changes by phone keeps your full limit.

1.We will pay your Direct Financial Loss resulting from Funds Transfer Fraud, Social Engineering Fraud or Invoice Manipulation that you first Discover during the Policy Period.

2.Callback verification. If Social Engineering Fraud involves an instruction to pay a new payee, or to change the bank account or payment details for an existing payee, the most we will pay for that Incident is $100,000 unless you performed Callback Verification before the transfer. This amount is part of, not in addition to, the Coverage H limit.

3.If Item 7 of the Declarations shows that the callback verification credit was not earned, the Coverage H limit is $100,000.

4.If you report the Incident to us, and to your financial institution, within 72 hours after you first Discover it, the reduced Retention shown in Item 6 applies.

INetwork Security and Privacy Liability

In shortIf customers, employees or business partners sue you after a breach or privacy failure, we defend you and pay settlements and judgments.

We will pay Damages and Claim Expenses that an Insured becomes legally obligated to pay because of a Claim alleging a Security Wrongful Act or Privacy Wrongful Act.

JRegulatory Defense and Penalties

In shortIf a regulator investigates you after a breach, we pay your lawyers and, where the law allows, the fines.

We will pay Claim Expenses and Regulatory Penalties that an Insured becomes legally obligated to pay because of a Regulatory Proceeding arising from a Security Failure or Privacy Event.

KPCI Fines and Assessments

In shortIf card data is breached and the card brands or your bank bill you for fines and fraud costs, we pay them.

We will pay PCI Fines and Assessments and Claim Expenses that an Insured becomes legally obligated to pay because of a PCI Claim arising from a Security Failure or Privacy Event involving Payment Card Data.

LMedia Liability

In shortIf someone sues you over content on your website, social media or marketing (for example for defamation or copyright infringement), we defend you and pay the damages.

We will pay Damages and Claim Expenses that an Insured becomes legally obligated to pay because of a Claim alleging a Media Wrongful Act.

Part 2 — Optional coverages (only if shown as purchased in Item 6)

MSystem Failure Business Interruption Optional

In shortExtends Coverage D to outages that are not caused by an attack, such as a failed software update or an IT mistake.

We will pay Business Interruption Loss that you incur during the Period of Restoration as a direct result of an Interruption of your Computer System caused by a System Failure that you first Discover during the Policy Period. We pay only if the Interruption lasts longer than the Waiting Period, and only for Business Interruption Loss incurred after the Waiting Period ends.

NComputer Replacement (Bricking) Optional

In shortIf an attack leaves laptops, servers or network equipment permanently unusable, we pay to replace them.

We will pay the reasonable cost to replace Hardware that a Security Failure, first Discovered during the Policy Period, has made unusable for its intended purpose, if the Hardware cannot be restored by reinstalling or reconfiguring its software or firmware, or if replacing it costs less than restoring it.

We will pay for replacement Hardware of like kind, quality and function. If that is no longer available, we will pay for the closest current equivalent, even if it performs better.

OReputational Harm Optional

In shortIf news of a breach drives customers away, we pay the profit you lose for up to 180 days.

We will pay Reputational Harm Loss that you incur during the 180 days after an Adverse Publication about a Security Failure or Privacy Event that you first Discover during the Policy Period. The Adverse Publication must first be published during the Policy Period or within 60 days after it ends.

PCryptojacking and Telecom Fraud Optional

In shortIf criminals use your computers or cloud accounts to mine cryptocurrency, or hijack your phone system, we pay the resulting bills.

We will pay the following amounts, if the Cryptojacking or Telecom Fraud is first Discovered during the Policy Period:

1.additional charges for electricity, cloud computing or other metered services that you incur because of Cryptojacking; and

2.charges from your telephone or telecommunications provider that you are legally obligated to pay because of Telecom Fraud.

QWebsite Tracking Liability Optional

In shortCovers lawsuits claiming that pixels, cookies or similar tools on your website collected or shared visitor data unlawfully.

We will pay Damages and Claim Expenses that an Insured becomes legally obligated to pay because of a Claim alleging a Tracking Wrongful Act. When this coverage applies, Exclusion III.A.15(b) does not apply to that Claim.

RIncreased Fraud Limit Optional

In shortDoubles the social engineering and funds transfer fraud limit to $500,000.

The Coverage H limit is increased to $500,000. The increased limit remains part of the Policy Aggregate Limit and remains subject to Coverage H, paragraphs 2 and 3.

Section II · Definitions

Section IIDefinitions

In this policy, words and phrases in bold have the meanings below. The singular includes the plural, and the plural includes the singular. “Including” means “including but not limited to.”

1.“Adverse Publication” means a report or communication to the public, by a news outlet or on a social media platform, by someone other than an Insured, that discloses a Security Failure or Privacy Event.

2.“Application” means the signed application for this policy (form HIC-CY-APP) and all information, attachments and statements you gave us with it or during underwriting. It does not include the results of any external security scan that we run ourselves.

3.“Bodily Injury” means physical injury, sickness, disease or death of a person, and any mental anguish or emotional distress resulting from it.

4.“Breach Response Costs” means the following reasonable and necessary costs, incurred within 12 months after you first Discover the Security Failure or Privacy Event (or longer if a law requires the service):

(a)forensic investigation to find the cause and scope of the event, contain it, and determine whether Personal Information or Confidential Information was affected;

(b)legal services to advise you on your response and to determine and meet your notification obligations under Privacy Law;

(c)notifying affected individuals, regulators, payment card companies and others as required by law, or as we agree will reduce Loss;

(d)a call center to answer questions from notified individuals;

(e)credit monitoring, identity monitoring and identity restoration services for affected individuals for 12 months, or longer if a law requires it; and

(f)public relations and crisis communications services to limit harm to your reputation.

5.“Business Interruption Loss” means Income Loss and Extra Expense. It also includes reasonable fees of a forensic accountant, approved by us, to calculate your Income Loss and Extra Expense.

6.“Callback Verification” means confirming a payment instruction, or a change to a payee's bank account or payment details, by contacting the person or organization that supposedly made the request, using a telephone number or other contact method that was on file before the request was received or that you obtained independently. A contact method supplied in the request itself does not count. You must keep a record of the verification.

7.“Claim” means any of the following made against an Insured:

(a)a written demand for money, services or other relief;

(b)a civil lawsuit, started by the service of a complaint or similar document;

(c)an arbitration, mediation or other alternative dispute resolution proceeding;

(d)a Regulatory Proceeding;

(e)a PCI Claim; or

(f)a written request to toll or waive a statute of limitations.

A Claim is first made when an Executive first receives it. A criminal proceeding is not a Claim.

8.“Claim Expenses” means reasonable and necessary fees and costs, including those of lawyers and experts, incurred by us, or by an Insured with our consent, to investigate, defend, settle or appeal a Claim, including the premium for an appeal bond (but not the obligation to provide one). It does not include salaries, wages or overhead of any Insured or of us.

9.“Computer System” means computers and related hardware, software, firmware, networks, mobile devices, connected devices, data storage and backup facilities, and cloud computing environments, that are:

(a)owned, leased or operated by the Insured Organization; or

(b)owned by an employee and used, with your permission, to conduct your business.

For Coverages B, C, F, I and J only, Computer System also includes systems operated for you by a third party under a written contract (for example, a hosted application or data storage service).

10.“Confidential Information” means non-public information of a third party, other than Personal Information, that is in the care, custody or control of an Insured (or of a third party on your behalf) and that you are legally or contractually required to keep confidential.

11.“Continuity Date” means the date shown in Item 8 of the Declarations.

12.“Cryptojacking” means the unauthorized use of your Computer System, including your cloud computing accounts, to mine digital currency.

13.“Cyber Extortion Threat” means a threat, made by someone other than an Executive, to do any of the following unless paid money, digital currency or other property: release, sell, destroy, corrupt or keep encrypted your Data or Personal Information; introduce malicious code into your Computer System; carry out a denial-of-service attack against your Computer System; or continue an attack already under way. It includes a Ransomware Event.

14.“Damages” means compensatory damages, judgments, settlements, pre-judgment and post-judgment interest, and awards of the claimant's legal costs, that an Insured is legally obligated to pay. It includes punitive, exemplary and multiplied damages where insurable under the law of the applicable jurisdiction that most favors coverage. It does not include:

(a)fines, penalties, taxes or sanctions, except as covered under Coverages J and K;

(b)the return or restitution of fees, profits or other amounts paid to an Insured;

(c)the cost of complying with an injunction or other non-monetary relief;

(d)royalties or licensing fees; or

(e)liquidated damages, to the extent they exceed the amount the Insured would have owed without the agreement.

15.“Data” means information that is stored electronically, including software, programs and electronic records.

16.“Deepfake” means audio, video, images or text created or altered using artificial intelligence or similar technology so that it appears to come from a real person or organization.

17.“Dependent Business” means an organization that is not an Insured and that provides you, under a written contract, with information technology services, including cloud computing (infrastructure, platform or software as a service), data hosting, managed IT or security services, or business software that you access online. It does not include a provider of electricity, gas, water, internet backbone, telecommunications or satellite services.

18.“Direct Financial Loss” means the amount of Money or Securities lost, or for Invoice Manipulation the amount your customer paid to the criminal that you are unable to collect, less any amount recovered. It does not include interest, lost profits, fees, or any other indirect or consequential loss.

19.“Discover” (and “Discovered” or “Discovery”) means the moment an Executive first becomes aware of facts that would cause a reasonable person to believe that an Incident has occurred or is occurring, even if the amount or details of the Loss are not yet known.

20.“Executive” means any owner, partner, principal, officer, director, member or manager of an Insured Organization, and the person responsible for information technology or information security, whether an employee or an outsourced provider acting in that role.

21.“Extended Reporting Period” means the period described in Section VI.D.

22.“Extortion Costs” means:

(a)fees of Panel Providers or other experts we approve to assess, negotiate and respond to a Cyber Extortion Threat;

(b)an Extortion Payment; and

(c)reasonable costs to buy, transfer or convert digital currency to make an Extortion Payment.

23.“Extortion Payment” means money, digital currency or other property paid to end a Cyber Extortion Threat, valued at the time of payment.

24.“Extra Expense” means reasonable and necessary costs, above your normal operating costs, that you incur to avoid or reduce an Interruption and to keep your business operating, such as overtime, temporary staff, rented equipment and outsourced services. It does not include Restoration Costs, Security Improvement Costs or the cost of improving any system.

25.“Funds Transfer Fraud” means a fraudulent instruction, sent to a financial institution by someone other than an Insured and without your knowledge or consent, that directs the institution to transfer, pay or deliver Money or Securities from an account held by the Insured Organization. It includes an instruction made through unauthorized access to your Computer System or online banking credentials.

26.“Hardware” means the physical components of your Computer System that the Insured Organization owns or leases, such as servers, desktop and laptop computers, network equipment and mobile devices.

27.“Hotline” means the incident reporting channels shown in Item 10 of the Declarations.

28.“Incident” means a Security Failure, Privacy Event, Cyber Extortion Threat, Funds Transfer Fraud, Social Engineering Fraud, Invoice Manipulation, System Failure, Cryptojacking or Telecom Fraud. Related Incidents are one Incident.

29.“Incident Response Services” means the following services from Panel Providers:

(a)a breach coach (a lawyer experienced in cyber incidents) to direct the response and protect legal privilege;

(b)initial forensic triage, including remote investigation and containment;

(c)an initial assessment of any Cyber Extortion Threat; and

(d)initial advice on your legal notification obligations.

30.“Income Loss” means the net profit before taxes that you would have earned if the Interruption had not occurred, minus the net profit before taxes you actually earned, plus your normal operating expenses (including payroll) that continue during the Interruption. It is calculated as Section V.I describes.

31.“Insured” means:

(a)the Insured Organization;

(b)any past, present or future Executive, employee, temporary or leased worker, intern or volunteer of the Insured Organization, but only while acting on its behalf;

(c)any independent contractor, but only while performing work for the Insured Organization under its direction;

(d)any person or organization that the Insured Organization agreed in a written contract to add as an insured, but only for its liability arising from a Wrongful Act of an Insured described in (a) to (c); and

(e)the spouse, domestic partner, estate, heirs or legal representatives of anyone in (b), but only for a Claim arising from that person's acts as an Insured.

32.“Insured Organization” means the Named Insured and any Subsidiary.

33.“Interruption” means an actual and measurable interruption, suspension, failure or slowdown of a computer system. It includes a reasonable shutdown of your Computer System that you make, or that we or a Panel Provider recommend, to contain or limit a Security Failure.

34.“Invoice Manipulation” means the sending of fraudulent payment instructions to one of your customers or clients, by a third party impersonating you, as a direct result of a Security Failure of your Computer System, which causes the customer or client to pay the third party money that it owed you for goods or services you had already provided.

35.“Loss” means any amount we agree to pay under a coverage in Section I, including Breach Response Costs, Business Interruption Loss, Claim Expenses, Damages, Direct Financial Loss, Extortion Costs, PCI Fines and Assessments, Regulatory Penalties, Reputational Harm Loss, Restoration Costs and Security Improvement Costs.

36.“Media Content” means text, images, audio, video, software code or other content that an Insured publishes or distributes, in any form or medium, including on your website, social media accounts, emails, newsletters and advertising.

37.“Media Wrongful Act” means any of the following committed by an Insured in Media Content: defamation, libel, slander, trade libel or product disparagement; invasion of privacy, false light, or misappropriation of a person's name or likeness; infringement of copyright, trademark, trade dress, domain name, title or slogan; plagiarism or piracy; or negligence in publishing content.

38.“Money” means currency, coins, bank notes and funds on deposit. It does not include digital currency.

39.“Named Insured” means the organization shown in Item 1 of the Declarations.

40.“Panel Provider” means a law firm, forensic firm, negotiator, notification vendor, public relations firm or other provider on our current list of approved providers, available through the Hotline.

41.“Payment Card Data” means cardholder data and sensitive authentication data, as those terms are defined in the PCI DSS.

42.“PCI Claim” means a written demand by a payment card brand, acquiring bank or payment processor for PCI Fines and Assessments under a merchant services or similar agreement. “PCI DSS” means the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard, as amended.

43.“PCI Fines and Assessments” means fines, penalties, fraud recoveries, operational reimbursements and case management fees that an Insured is required to pay under a merchant services or similar agreement because of actual or alleged non-compliance with the PCI DSS. It does not include interchange fees, chargebacks, the cost of becoming compliant, or fines for continued non-compliance after the Security Failure or Privacy Event has been fixed.

44.“Period of Restoration” means the period that begins when the Interruption begins and ends on the earlier of:

(a)the date the affected computer system is restored, or with reasonable speed could have been restored, to the same condition and function it had before the Interruption, plus the time reasonably needed for your income to return to the level it would have reached had no Interruption occurred; or

(b)180 days after the Interruption began.

45.“Personal Information” means information that identifies, or can reasonably be linked to, an individual, including names combined with Social Security numbers, taxpayer identification numbers, driver's license or passport numbers, financial account or payment card numbers, login credentials, health information and biometric data, and any other information protected by a Privacy Law. It does not include information lawfully made available to the public by a government.

46.“Policy Period” means the period shown in Item 2 of the Declarations, or any shorter period if this policy is cancelled.

47.“Privacy Event” means:

(a)the actual or reasonably suspected unauthorized access to, or acquisition, disclosure, loss or use of, Personal Information or Confidential Information in the care, custody or control of an Insured, or of a third party on your behalf, however it happens (including by the loss or theft of a device or paper records, or an employee's mistake); or

(b)an unintentional violation of a Privacy Law by an Insured.

48.“Privacy Law” means any law or regulation that governs the security, confidentiality or privacy of Personal Information, including data breach notification laws, the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and the Federal Trade Commission's Safeguards Rule, the California Consumer Privacy Act, and the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulations. It does not include any law described in Exclusion III.A.15 or III.A.16.

49.“Privacy Wrongful Act” means any actual or alleged act, error or omission by an Insured (or by a third party for whom an Insured is legally responsible) that results in a Privacy Event, including a failure to give timely notice of a Privacy Event as a Privacy Law requires, or a failure to follow your own published privacy policy.

50.“Ransomware Event” means a Security Failure in which malicious software encrypts, locks or otherwise makes Data or your Computer System unavailable, whether or not a demand for payment is made.

51.“Regulatory Penalties” means civil fines and penalties imposed on an Insured in a Regulatory Proceeding, and amounts an Insured must deposit into a consumer redress fund, where insurable under the law of the applicable jurisdiction that most favors coverage.

52.“Regulatory Proceeding” means a civil investigation, demand for information, administrative proceeding or civil lawsuit brought against an Insured by a government agency or regulator (including a state attorney general, the Federal Trade Commission, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, or a foreign data protection authority), started by a written notice received by an Executive.

53.“Related” means arising from the same, continuous or causally connected facts, circumstances, events or Wrongful Acts. All Related Incidents are one Incident, first Discovered when the earliest was first Discovered. All Related Claims are one Claim, first made when the earliest was first made.

54.“Reputational Harm Loss” means the net profit before taxes that you lose as a direct result of an Adverse Publication, calculated as Section V.I describes for Income Loss. It does not include Breach Response Costs or any Business Interruption Loss.

55.“Restoration Costs” means reasonable and necessary costs to:

(a)restore, recreate or recollect Data and software to the condition they were in before the Security Failure, from backups or original sources;

(b)find and remove malicious code; and

(c)determine that Data cannot be restored or recreated, if that is the case.

It does not include the value of the Data, the cost of creating it originally, or the cost of improving any system.

56.“Retention” means the amount shown in Items 5 and 6 of the Declarations that you must pay for each Incident or Claim before we pay, as Section IV.D describes.

57.“Retroactive Date” means the date shown in Item 8 of the Declarations. If Item 8 shows “Full prior acts,” no Retroactive Date applies.

58.“Securities” means negotiable and non-negotiable instruments or contracts that represent money or other property, not including Money or digital currency.

59.“Security Failure” means a failure or breach of the security of your Computer System (or, for Coverage E, of a Dependent Business's computer systems), including:

(a)unauthorized access to or use of the system, including with stolen credentials or credentials obtained by deception;

(b)a denial-of-service attack;

(c)the introduction or transmission of malicious code, including ransomware; and

(d)the loss or theft of Hardware containing Data.

60.“Security Improvement Costs” means reasonable costs of software, services and configuration (including multi-factor authentication, endpoint detection and response, managed detection and response for up to 12 months, and security awareness training) that a Panel Provider recommends in writing to prevent a similar Incident. It does not include Hardware, the salaries of your employees, or costs that you had already committed to before the Incident.

61.“Security Wrongful Act” means any actual or alleged act, error or omission by an Insured (or by a third party for whom an Insured is legally responsible) that results in a Security Failure, including a failure that allows malicious code to pass from your Computer System to a third party's systems, or that prevents authorized users from accessing your Computer System.

62.“Social Engineering Fraud” means the intentional deception of an Insured by someone impersonating a vendor, client, Executive, employee, financial institution or other trusted party, through email, telephone, text message, video call or other communication (including a Deepfake), which causes the Insured to transfer Money or Securities of the Insured Organization, or to change a payee's bank account or payment details, so that funds are paid to someone other than the intended recipient.

63.“Subsidiary” means an organization in which the Named Insured owns, directly or indirectly, more than 50% of the voting rights on the first day of the Policy Period, or that it acquires during the Policy Period as Section VI.G describes.

64.“System Failure” means an unintentional and unplanned outage of your Computer System caused by human error, a programming error, a failed software update or patch (including one issued by a vendor), or a malfunction of software or Hardware. It does not include a Security Failure.

65.“Telecom Fraud” means the unauthorized access to or use of your telephone or telecommunications systems by someone other than an Insured, resulting in charges to you.

66.“Tracking Wrongful Act” means the actual or alleged collection, interception, recording or sharing of information about visitors to your website or users of your online services through pixels, cookies, web beacons, session replay, chat tools or similar technology without the consent a law requires, in violation of a wiretapping, eavesdropping, video privacy or consumer privacy law.

67.“Waiting Period” means the number of hours shown for the applicable coverage in Item 6 of the Declarations, counted from when the Interruption begins.

68.“Wrongful Act” means a Security Wrongful Act, Privacy Wrongful Act, Media Wrongful Act or, if Coverage Q applies, a Tracking Wrongful Act.
