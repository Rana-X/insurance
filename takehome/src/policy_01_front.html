Important Notices

Important Notices to Policyholders

Please read these notices. They explain how this policy works and what it does not do. They do not change or add to the coverage in the policy. If a notice and the policy wording differ, the policy wording controls.

1

Some coverages are claims-made and reported

Coverages I (Network Security and Privacy Liability), J (Regulatory Defense and Penalties), K (PCI Fines and Assessments), L (Media Liability) and, if purchased, Q (Website Tracking Liability) cover only Claims first made against you during the Policy Period and reported to us within the deadlines in Section V. A Claim first made after this policy ends is not covered unless an Extended Reporting Period applies. If you replace this policy, make sure the new policy covers earlier events (see “Retroactive Date” in Item 8 of the Declarations).

2

Defense costs reduce your limits

Money we spend on lawyers, experts and other Claim Expenses is part of, and reduces, the limits of liability. It also counts toward your Retention. Once the limits are used up, we have no further duty to defend or pay.

3

How to read this policy

We have tried to write this policy in plain English. Words in bold have the meanings given in Section II, Definitions. The coverage letters (A through R) are the same in the Declarations and the policy wording. Headings and the shaded “In short” notes help you find your way. They are not part of the contract terms and do not limit or expand coverage.

4

What this policy does not do

This policy is not a guarantee that you will not suffer a cyberattack, and it does not replace good security. It does not cover every cyber-related loss. In particular, it is not a professional liability (errors and omissions) policy, a crime or fidelity policy for employee theft, or a property policy for physical damage. You may need those policies as well. Ask your insurance adviser if you are unsure.

5

Ransom payments and sanctions

We may pay or reimburse an extortion payment only where the law allows it, only with our prior written consent, and only after the checks described in Section V. We cannot make or reimburse any payment that would violate U.S. or other applicable economic sanctions. U.S. Department of the Treasury guidance encourages victims to report ransomware attacks to law enforcement promptly. We will help you do so.

6

Terrorism coverage

Losses from certified acts of terrorism are covered to the extent this policy otherwise covers them. The required federal disclosure is included at the end of this packet (form HIC-IL-TRIA).

7

Your privacy

We collect information about your business, and about individuals connected to it, to underwrite this policy, handle claims and meet legal obligations. We do not sell this information. We share it only with service providers, reinsurers, regulators and others as the law permits or requires. You may ask what information we hold about you by contacting us at the address in Item 10 of the Declarations.

8

Admitted insurer and guaranty fund protection

Harborline Insurance Company is licensed (“admitted”) to write this insurance in Colorado, and this policy form is filed with the Colorado Division of Insurance. If an admitted insurer becomes insolvent, covered claims may be paid by the state insurance guaranty association, subject to its limits and rules.

9

Questions and complaints

If you have a question about your coverage or a claim, call us first at 1-800-555-0142. If we cannot resolve your concern, you may contact the Colorado Division of Insurance (doi.colorado.gov).

What to Do If Something Happens

What to do if something happens

Keep this page where your team can find it. Speed matters: most of the cost of an attack is decided in the first few hours.

1

Call the hotline first: 1-800-555-0142

Our breach coach answers 24/7. The first 72 hours of incident response are free (Coverage A) and do not reduce your limit. If you are unsure whether something counts, call anyway. Suspected incidents are covered.

2

Don't pay a ransom, negotiate or admit fault before talking to us

Paying or negotiating without our consent can break the law and can void coverage for that payment. Don't reply to the attacker. Don't tell customers or others that you are at fault.

3

Preserve evidence

Disconnect affected machines from the network, but don't switch them off, wipe or rebuild them until our forensics team says so. Keep emails, logs and ransom notes.

4

Sent money to a fraudster? Call your bank, then us, within 72 hours

Ask your bank to recall the transfer and file a report at ic3.gov. Reporting to us within 72 hours halves your retention for Coverage H to $2,500, and fast reporting gives the best chance of recovering the money.

5

We assemble your response team

Lawyers, forensic investigators, negotiators and notification vendors from our panel start work without further paperwork. You may ask to use your own providers. See Section V.C of the policy.

6

Make legally required notices on time

Many state laws, including Colorado's, require you to notify affected people within 30 days. You never need our permission to meet a legal deadline, and we will never deny coverage because you did.
