## Top findings (read these first)

1. **Late notice goes further than Colorado law requires.** Under *Craft v. Philadelphia Indemnity* (Colo. 2015), the notice-prejudice rule does **not** apply to the date-certain reporting deadline in a claims-made policy. Harborline V.1.3 ("If you report late, we will reduce what we pay only to the extent the delay actually harmed…") does not separate the "as soon as practicable" duty (V.1.1) from the outer 90-day deadline (V.1.2). Read against the drafter, it gives up the protection *Craft* allows and turns claims-made-and-reported into an open-ended tail. Fix it, or keep it on purpose and price it.
2. **The consent clause is enforced strictly in Colorado.** In *Travelers v. Stresscon* (Colo. 2016, 4–3), the court held that an insurer need not prove prejudice to enforce a no-voluntary-payments clause [S holding; K name/cite]. As a result, Harborline V.2.2 is harsher in practice than the policy's own promises. It also conflicts with III.2.5 and with the "with our consent" wording in the definition of breach response costs.
3. **The fraud cancellation notice is too short for Colorado.** C.R.S. 10-4-109.7 requires **45 days'** notice of intent to cancel covered commercial lines, and 10 days for non-payment with reasons [S]. Harborline's 30 days for fraud (V.5.2) falls short. The 10 days for non-payment and the 60-day non-renewal notice are fine.
4. **Punitive damages will probably not be paid for a Colorado insured.** Colorado public policy bars insuring punitive damages (*Lira v. Shelter*, Colo. 1996) [S]. The policy also contradicts itself: the damages definition says "most favorable law", III.7.5 says "applicable law" and Item 11 says "Colorado". Pick one rule and state it once.
5. **Arbitration is permitted in Colorado for this line.** The Colorado statute that voids mandatory arbitration, C.R.S. 10-3-1116(3) as applied in *Meardon* (2018 COA 32), covers only **health, life and disability** policies [S]. "Binding where state law allows" is still vague about who is bound, when, under which rules, and whether statutory bad-faith claims are included.
6. **The service standards create bad-faith evidence.** C.R.S. 10-3-1115/1116 treat business insureds as first-party claimants, including for benefits paid "on behalf of" the insured, and allow two times the covered benefit plus attorney fees [S/K]. Harborline's published 30-day and 15-day targets will likely become the yardstick for "unreasonable delay". "Statutory rate" should name C.R.S. 5-12-102 (8% compounded annually, at the claimant's election) [S]. The clause should also say the interest adds to statutory remedies and does not replace them.
7. **The TRIA notice is deficient.** Stand-alone cyber policies are "property and casualty insurance" under TRIA (Treasury guidance, Dec. 27, 2016) [S]. The required disclosure must state the **80% federal share** and the **$100B cap** [S]. Notice 7 says only "subject to the program's limits", and Item 12 points back to Notice 7, which is circular.
8. **Application 7.5 ("No") is correct, but the reason given is incomplete.** The Colorado Privacy Act's 25,000-consumer prong applies only to controllers that **derive revenue or a discount from selling personal data** [S]. The CPA also keeps an **entity-level GLBA exemption**; I found no Colorado amendment narrowing it, unlike Montana (effective Oct. 1, 2025) and Connecticut (effective July 1, 2026) [S]. Round 1 said the ~27,300 Colorado residents put Cedar Ridge "above the CPA's 25,000 prong"; that overstates the exposure.
9. **Regulatory status changes since the package was written:**
   - **SB 690 retroactivity.** SB 690 (CIPA) would make website/app pen-register and trap-and-trace claims enforceable by the Attorney General only, retroactive to pending actions filed within two years before its Jan. 1, 2027 operative date. The Governor's deadline is Sept. 30, 2026 [S].
   - **BIPA per-person damages retroactive.** The Seventh Circuit held in *Clay v. Union Pacific* (Apr. 2026) that the 2024 BIPA per-person damages amendment applies retroactively [S].
   - **CIRCIA.** The final rule was still only targeted for "September 2026" in the latest snippets [S].
   - **HIPAA Security Rule.** Moved to the long-term agenda, with final action estimated July 2027 [S].
10. **Case-law lessons for specific clauses:**
    - "Direct result" wording in *funds transfer loss* is the exact phrase litigated in *Apache*, *Medidata* and *American Tooling*.
    - *CiCi Enterprises v. HSB* (N.D. Tex. Feb. 23, 2026) refused to let a ransomware sublimit cap cyber-extortion coverage [S]. Harborline's ransomware coinsurance (III.1.6) needs an explicit scope.
    - The UDAP exclusion (IV.2.8) will bite on consumer-protection counts pleaded in breach and pixel class actions.
11. **Correction to the brief.** *Principle Solutions Group v. **Ironshore** Indemnity*, 944 F.3d 886 (11th Cir. 2019) [K]. The insurer was Ironshore, not ACE.

## Part A. Colorado law that bears on the wording

### A1. Notice-prejudice, and whether it reaches claims-made-and-reported deadlines

| Authority | Holding / status | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Clementi v. Nationwide Mut. Fire Ins. Co.*, 16 P.3d 223 (Colo. 2001) | Adopted the notice-prejudice rule in a first-party uninsured/underinsured motorist setting. The insurer must show prejudice from late notice. | [S] https://blogs.duanemorris.com/insurancelaw/tag/clementi/ |
| *Friedland v. Travelers Indem. Co.*, 105 P.3d 639 (Colo. 2005) | Extended notice-prejudice to occurrence-based **liability** policies. Where the insured gave notice only after defending and settling, the delay is unreasonable as a matter of law and prejudice is **presumed**. | [S] https://law.justia.com/cases/colorado/supreme-court/2005/03sc681-0.html |
| *Craft v. Philadelphia Indem. Ins. Co.*, 2015 CO 11, 343 P.3d 951 (decided Feb. 17, 2015) | Notice-prejudice does **not** apply to a **date-certain** notice requirement in a claims-made policy. The policy there required notice "as soon as practicable" **and** no later than 60 days after expiry. The date-certain deadline defines the "temporal boundaries" of coverage, and excusing it "would defeat the fundamental concept on which coverage is premised." | [S] https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2823833/craft-v-philadelphia-indemnity-insurance-co/ ; https://www.hallevans.com/colorado-supreme-court-rejects-extending-notice-prejudice-rule-to-claims-made-policies/ ; https://www.jonesgrahamkelly.com/blog/colorado-high-court-refuses-to-apply-notice-prejudice-rule-to-date-certain-notice-requirement-in-claims-made-policy/ |
| *Travelers Prop. Cas. Co. of Am. v. Stresscon Corp.*, 2016 CO 22 (Colo. Apr. 2016), No. 13SC815 | 4–3 decision: notice-prejudice does **not** apply when the insured makes voluntary payments in breach of a "no voluntary payments" clause. | Holding [S]: https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=669aff76-ff45-4291-9399-8f93a188eb27 ; https://blogs.duanemorris.com/insurancelaw/2016/04/28/colorado-supreme-court-holds-that-insurer-need-not-prove-prejudice-to-enforce-no-voluntary-payments-clause/ . Case name and "2016 CO 22" [K] |

**What this means for Harborline**

- **V.1.3 on claims.** V.1.1 is the "as soon as practicable" duty; V.1.2 is the date-certain deadline of "no later than 90 days after the policy period ends". Colorado would let Harborline enforce V.1.2 strictly for claims (*Craft*). V.1.3 is unqualified ("If you report late…"), so a Colorado court applying contra proferentem could read it to reach V.1.2 too. On that reading, a claim first reported a year after expiry is covered unless Harborline proves prejudice. That is a contractual waiver of *Craft*: an unpriced tail, and a problem for reinsurers.
- **V.1.3 on first-party incidents** (A–H, M–P, S). Colorado has not squarely decided whether a date-certain outer deadline for a discovered first-party loss is enforceable without prejudice after *Clementi* [U]. Applying a prejudice standard there is consistent with *Clementi*, and it is a defensible, insured-friendly choice.
- **Reporting windows overlap.** The 90-day reporting window (V.1.2) and the 60-day automatic ERP (V.5.4, Item 9) cover different things: claims made during the period versus claims first made after expiry. V.1.2's "or by the end of any extended reporting period" does not say which applies, or whether the later date wins.
- **Consent clause.** *Stresscon* means the consent clause in V.2.2 ("Do not … settle a claim or agree to pay costs without our consent") is enforceable without prejudice in Colorado. That is stricter than the "prejudice only" tone of the package.

### A2. Rescission and misrepresentation standard

| Authority | Holding | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Hollinger v. Mutual Benefit Life Ins. Co.*, 192 Colo. 377, 560 P.2d 824 (1977) | To rescind, the insurer must prove five things: (1) a false statement or concealment in the application; (2) made **knowingly**; (3) **material** to acceptance of the risk or the hazard assumed; (4) the insurer was ignorant of the falsity and "**not chargeable with knowledge**"; and (5) the insurer relied on it to its detriment. No separate "intent to deceive" is required. | [S] https://law.justia.com/cases/colorado/supreme-court/1977/c-793.html ; https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1221876/hollinger-v-mutual-benefit-life-insurance/ |
| *Travelers Prop. Cas. Co. of Am. v. International Control Services, Inc.*, No. 22-cv-2145 (C.D. Ill. 2022) | The insurer sued to rescind after ransomware, over an MFA answer. The parties stipulated to rescission from inception (Aug. 2022). Illinois law; not a merits ruling. | [C] rationale "Sections IV and V checks" |

**What this means for Harborline**

- **V.3.3 sets a higher bar than Colorado law.** "Knowingly **and intentionally**", by an **executive**, is stricter than *Hollinger*, which needs only "knowingly". That is lawful, because the contract can give more than the minimum.
- **V.3.2 leaves the "would have declined" case open.** The proportionate remedy ("apply the premium and terms we would have offered") says nothing about an honest but material error where Harborline would have **declined** the risk entirely. V.5.2 then bars cancellation for anything other than non-payment or fraud, so Harborline would be locked in for the term. That may be intended; if so, say it.
- **V.3.3 imputation is broad for a small business.** "Executive" includes "the person responsible for your information technology or security", and an executive's knowledge is imputed to the named insured. So an IT manager's knowing misstatement can rescind the firm's own coverage. Cedar Ridge's IT manager co-signed the application. That may be the intended result, but it cuts against the "innocent insureds" theme.

Cyber is not one of the lines named in the snippet [U]. Because Harborline sells liability and E&O-type coverage, treat 10-4-109.7 as applying.

**What this means for Harborline**

- V.5.2's **30 days for fraud is below the 45-day minimum.** The conformity clause (V.11.5) and the unreproduced Colorado amendatory endorsement HIC-CY-CO would repair this, but the specimen shows a number that does not comply in its own chosen state.
- **10 days for non-payment is compliant**, provided the notice states the reason.
- **60 days for non-renewal exceeds 45.**
- **Delivery method.** The application consents to electronic notices. Whether a Colorado cancellation notice can go electronically instead of by first-class mail depends on Colorado's e-delivery rules [U].

### A4. Binding arbitration in Colorado insurance contracts

| Authority | Content | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C.R.S. 10-3-1116(3); *Meardon v. Freedom Life Ins. Co. of Am.*, 2018 COA 32 (Mar. 8, 2018) | A policy issued in Colorado must let a person claiming **health, life or disability** benefits seek de novo review in court with a jury. A mandatory arbitration clause was held unenforceable for those claims. McCarran-Ferguson reverse-preempts the FAA on this point. | [S] https://www.burgsimpson.com/colorado-blog/appeals-mandatory-arbitration/ ; https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4476157/meardon-v-freedom-life-insurance/ ; statute: https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/2021/title-10/article-3/part-11/section-10-3-1116/ |
| Colorado Uniform Arbitration Act, C.R.S. 13-22-201 et seq. | Generally enforces written arbitration agreements. | [K] |

**What this means for Harborline:** 10-3-1116(3) does **not** reach a commercial cyber policy, so Colorado allows binding arbitration here. The problems are in the drafting:

- "Binding where state law allows" does not say binding **on whom** or **from when**.
- It names no rules, seat or fee allocation.
- It does not say whether statutory bad-faith claims under 10-3-1116 are included.
- It does not say whether Harborline may refuse the election.

The form is pitched at businesses nationwide. Several states restrict pre-dispute arbitration in insurance contracts [K; list not verified]. An election made **after the dispute arises** and only by the insured avoids most of those statutes.

### A5. Insurability of punitive damages and regulatory fines

| Authority | Content | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Lira v. Shelter Ins. Co.*, 913 P.2d 514 (Colo. 1996) | Colorado public policy prohibits insurance coverage for punitive damages. An insured cannot recover from its insurer, as bad-faith damages, the punitive award entered against it. | [S] https://law.justia.com/cases/colorado/supreme-court/1996/95sc153-0.html |
| Tenth Circuit decision (name **[U]**) | Per a snippet, held that TCPA statutory damages are penalties and not insurable as a matter of Colorado public policy. | [S] https://www.plunkettcooney.com/publications-Punitive-Damages-Lost-Policies-Preemption-Coverage-Update (fetch blocked; case not identified) |
| Colorado choice-of-law approach | Colorado follows the Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws for contracts. A chosen law yields where it conflicts with the forum's fundamental policy. | [K] |

**What this means for Harborline**

- **Punitive damages.** Cedar Ridge is a Colorado insured, most suits against it will be in Colorado, and Item 11 picks Colorado law. Punitive damages are therefore very likely **not payable**, whatever the "most favorable law" wording says. The package should say this plainly instead of implying broad cover.
- **Internal conflict.** The damages definition says "most favorable to coverage", III.7.5 says "only where insurable under applicable law", and Item 11 says Colorado. That is a three-way inconsistency.
- **Regulatory fines.** I located no Colorado appellate decision on whether privacy or data-security civil penalties are insurable [U]. The TCPA snippet suggests Colorado courts may treat penal amounts as uninsurable. Coverage J's "where insurable" hedge is therefore necessary, but it gives Cedar Ridge little certainty.
- **"Consumer redress."** The regulatory penalties definition includes "consumer redress amounts". Redress is often characterized as restitution, which is uninsurable in some courts (e.g., *Level 3 Commc'ns v. Federal Ins. Co.*, 272 F.3d 908 (7th Cir. 2001) [K]). It also sits awkwardly with damages exclusion (2) ("restitution") and exclusion IV.2.19 ("profit or advantage you were not legally entitled to").

### A6. Bad faith and unreasonable delay, and how the service standards interact

| Authority | Content | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C.R.S. 10-3-1115 | An insurer shall not unreasonably delay or deny payment of a covered benefit to a first-party claimant. "First-party claimant" means "an individual, corporation, association, partnership, or other legal entity asserting an entitlement to benefits owed directly to **or on behalf of** an insured". A delay or denial is unreasonable if made "without a reasonable basis". | [S] https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_10-3-1115 |
| C.R.S. 10-3-1116(1) | A first-party claimant may recover reasonable attorney fees, court costs and **two times the covered benefit**. | [K] (statute URL above, A4) |
| C.R.S. 5-12-102 | For money wrongfully withheld, interest is either the gain realized by the withholder or, at the claimant's election, **8% per annum compounded annually**. | [S] https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_5-12-102 |
| C.R.S. 10-3-1104(1)(h) | Unfair claim settlement practices, used as evidence of the standard of care. | [K] |

**What this means for Harborline**

- **The targets will set the standard.** V.7.2 ("aim to give … a written coverage position … within 30 days") and V.7.3 ("aim to pay undisputed amounts within 15 days … interest at the statutory rate") are phrased as aims. They will still be the natural benchmark for "reasonable" under 10-3-1115. Missing them invites a 10-3-1116 claim for two times the benefit. That is good for insureds, but Harborline should accept it knowingly.
- **"Statutory rate" is ambiguous in Colorado.** 5-12-102 offers gain-realized or 8%, and judgments carry separate rates.
- **Interest must not read as an exclusive remedy.** Without a saving sentence, the interest clause could be argued to be an exclusive remedy for late payment. It could not lawfully displace 10-3-1116, and the argument itself looks bad.
- **The insurer controls both clocks.** "After we agree on them" and "undisputed" make the 15-day clock start only when Harborline agrees. "After receiving the documents we ask for" does the same for the 30-day clock.
- **Defense counts.** Defense under Coverage I is a benefit "on behalf of" the insured, so slow defense funding is also exposed to 10-3-1116.

### A8. Colorado breach notice (C.R.S. 6-1-716) and data security (C.R.S. 6-1-713.5)

- **6-1-716 deadlines.** Notice to affected Colorado residents **no later than 30 days** after determining that a breach occurred [S]. Notice to the Attorney General if **500 or more** Colorado residents are affected, also within 30 days, and to consumer reporting agencies if more than 1,000 [K]. Sources: https://privacyrights.org/resources-tools/reports/data-breach-notification-laws-50-state-survey-2026-edition ; https://hodder.law/state-data-breach-notification-laws-2026/
- **Conflict rule.** How 6-1-716 interacts with another law's timeline (the "shortest time frame controls" language) is **[U]**.
- **6-1-713.5.** Requires reasonable security procedures appropriate to the nature of the personal information and the size of the business, and requires that third-party service providers be bound to the same (HB 18-1128, eff. Sept. 1, 2018) [K].

**What this means for Harborline:** About 88% of 31,000 individuals (≈27,300) are Colorado residents (application 3.2–3.3) [C]. A full breach would therefore trigger all of the following, each on 30-day clocks:

- notice to affected Colorado residents;
- notice to the Colorado Attorney General;
- notice to the consumer reporting agencies; and
- notice to the FTC under the Safeguards Rule (B2).

V.1.5 and III.2.5 ("Legal deadlines come first") are therefore essential. They must not be undercut by the "with our consent" wording in the definition of breach response costs.

**What this means for Harborline:** Cedar Ridge does not sell data (application 3.9) and has fewer than 100,000 Colorado consumers, so it is **below the thresholds**. Its ~27,300 Colorado residents do not meet the 25,000 prong without sale revenue, and employees do not count as consumers. It is also **exempt at entity level** as a GLBA financial institution, since tax preparers are financial institutions under the FTC Safeguards Rule [C]. The 7.5 answer "No" is right; its stated reason should cite both grounds. One caution: GA4 behind a consent banner (3.8) could, under some state laws, be argued to be a "sale" or "sharing". The entity exemption makes that moot in Colorado.

### A10. Colorado AI Act status

**SB 24-205 was repealed and re-enacted by SB 26-189**, which Gov. Polis **signed May 14, 2026**, **effective Jan. 1, 2027** (confirmed) [S]. Sources: https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/colorado-governor-signs-sb-189 ; https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/colorado-enacts-artificial-intelligence-replacement-law.html ; https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/colorado-replaces-landmark-ai-act-an-overview-of-the-new-sb-26-189-framework.html ; https://legiscan.com/CO/bill/SB189/2026

- **What the new law covers.** It regulates automated decision-making technology (ADMT) used in consequential decisions, including employment.
- **Deployer duties.** Notice before use, an explanation of adverse outcomes within 30 days, and meaningful human review where commercially reasonable.
- **What it drops.** No mandatory NIST or ISO risk-management program, no annual impact assessments, and no duty to self-report algorithmic discrimination to the Attorney General.

**What this means for Harborline:** Attorney General enforcement under SB 26-189 is a "regulatory proceeding" only if it arises from a **security failure or privacy event**. Most AI-disclosure or discrimination enforcement will not, so Coverage J will not respond. The affirmative-AI promise (Section I; IV.1) should not be marketed as AI-compliance cover. The rationale's status row is accurate.

## Part C. Coverage case law that informs specific clauses

### C1. War and state-backed operations

- ***Merck & Co. v. ACE Am. Ins. Co.*, 475 N.J. Super. 420 (App. Div. May 1, 2023).**
  - The "hostile or warlike action" exclusion is limited to traditional military action and did not bar NotPetya losses of about $1.4B [K on amount and date; C on citation].
  - The insurers' appeal to the NJ Supreme Court was **dismissed after settlement** [S] https://insidecybersecurity.com/daily-news/insurers-appeal-ruling-case-over-war-exclusion-applicability-cyber-events ; the settlement was reported in Jan. 2024 [K].
- ***Mondelez Int'l v. Zurich Am. Ins. Co.*** (Ill. Cir. Ct., Cook County). Settled during trial in **late Oct./Nov. 2022**, with no ruling on the war exclusion [K; docket number [U]].
- **Lloyd's Market Bulletin Y5381 (Aug. 2022)** requires state-backed cyber-attack exclusions with an attribution mechanism, from Mar. 31, 2023 [K; C].
- **Lesson for Harborline.** Legacy "hostilities/warlike" wording is unreliable, and Harborline's modern exclusion is the right direction. Two points to note:
  - Colorado construes exclusions narrowly, with the insurer bearing the burden [K]. Harborline states that burden expressly (IV.2.15.4), which is consistent.
  - The bystander carve-back (IV.2.15.2) protects only systems **outside** the impacted state. If the United States is the state that suffers the major detrimental impact, a Denver insured's losses remain excluded. That is by design, but the "Cyber terrorism and state-linked criminals" box in IV.1 may lead Cedar Ridge to expect more.

### C2. Rescission over MFA answers

*Travelers v. ICS* (C.D. Ill. 2022) is discussed in A2. The lesson is already reflected in V.3. Colorado's *Hollinger* "not chargeable with knowledge" element also supports the scan estoppel (V.3.4), but only for facts the scan actually made knowable.

### C3. Social engineering, computer fraud and "direct loss"

| Case | Holding | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Apache Corp. v. Great Am. Ins. Co.*, 662 F. App'x 252 (5th Cir. Oct. 18, 2016) | No computer-fraud coverage. The email was incidental; the loss resulted from other steps, including employee action. | [K] |
| *Medidata Solutions v. Federal Ins. Co.*, 729 F. App'x 117 (2d Cir. July 6, 2018) | Coverage under computer fraud for a spoofed-email wire. The court rejected the "hacking only" and "no direct loss" arguments. | [S, holding] https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=055ab466-5b12-402a-b2a2-7b1d375b6ffa ; cite [K] |
| *American Tooling Ctr. v. Travelers Cas. & Sur. Co. of Am.*, 895 F.3d 455 (6th Cir. July 13, 2018) | "Direct" means immediate; the loss was covered despite intervening employee steps. | [K] |
| *Principle Solutions Grp. v. **Ironshore** Indem.*, 944 F.3d 886 (11th Cir. 2019) | Coverage for an executive-impersonation wire under the "fraudulent instruction" / computer-fraud wording. The brief's "v. ACE" appears to be an error. | [K] |
| *Interactive Commc'ns Int'l v. Great Am.* (11th Cir. 2018) | No coverage; loss not "direct". | [K; cite U] |
| *G&G Oil Co. of Ind. v. Continental W. Ins. Co.* (Ind. 2021) | Ransomware payment covered under computer fraud as "fraudulently cause a transfer". | [K; cite U] |
| *Bridlewood Estates POA v. State Farm Gen. Ins. Co.* (S.D. Cal. Mar. 18, 2024) | Vendor-impersonation wire; social-engineering coverage issue. Holding details were not captured. | [S] https://www.ecjlaw.com/ecj-blog/a-new-twist-on-coverage-for-losses-from-spoofed-emails |

Survey of 2024 decisions: https://litchfieldcavo.com/insurance-coverage-for-funds-transfer-scams-six-recent-cases/ [S].

**What this means for Harborline**

- **"Direct result" is the disputed phrase.** The *funds transfer loss* definition uses "as a **direct result** of a fraudulent instruction or computer fraud", the phrase fought over in *Apache*, *Medidata* and *American Tooling*. Because Harborline intends to cover employee-induced transfers, use "resulting from", or add "including where an employee's authorized act was induced by the fraud".
- **Round-1 gap confirmed.** Fraudulent instruction item (3) requires that the instruction "leads an **employee** to transfer". Instructions sent straight to the bank therefore fit only under "computer fraud", and only if a computer-fraud event occurred. III.6.1's statement that the reduced limit "does not apply when criminals send instructions directly to your bank" implies cover that item (3) does not grant.

### C4. Ransomware and "physical loss" under property forms

***EMOI Services, L.L.C. v. Owners Ins. Co.*, 2022-Ohio-4649 (Ohio Dec. 2022).** Software and data are intangible, so ransomware encryption was not "direct physical loss or damage" under a business-owners form's media endorsement [K].

**For Harborline:** this supports stand-alone cyber data-restoration and BI cover (F, D). Exclusion IV.2.1 ("physical damage to tangible property") should not reach data. It does not, as drafted: data is not tangible property, and Coverage M has a carve-back. No change needed. Consider adding "digital assets are not tangible property" to remove any doubt.

### C5. PCI assessments

- ***P.F. Chang's China Bistro v. Federal Ins. Co.*** (D. Ariz. May 2016): card-brand assessments passed through a processor fell within the cyber policy's contractual-liability exclusion [K].
- ***Landry's, Inc. v. Ins. Co. of the State of Pa.*, 4 F.4th 366 (5th Cir. 2021):** a CGL "publication" of private information triggered a duty to defend the processor's suit seeking card-brand assessments [K].

**For Harborline:** the express Coverage K plus the PCI carve-back from the contract exclusion (IV.2.5) is exactly the fix P.F. Chang's calls for. It works as intended.

### C6. Data-breach class actions and UDAP counts

Consumer breach and pixel class actions routinely plead state UDAP or UCL counts alongside negligence and implied contract. Examples include Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §17200 and the Colorado Consumer Protection Act, C.R.S. 6-1-105 [K]. I located no specific coverage decision applying a cyber-form UDAP exclusion to a breach class action this session [U].

**For Harborline:** IV.2.8 excludes "unfair or deceptive trade practices" with a carve-back **only** for regulatory proceedings. In a Coverage I class action, the UDAP count is excluded. III.7.4 (mixed claims) keeps defense at 100% until the covered allegations resolve, but then:

- settlement allocation can carve out the UDAP share; and
- if the negligence counts are dismissed and the UDAP count survives, defense ends.

The Section IV lead-in ("arising from") widens this further. See D-11.

### C7. Hammer clause and duty to defend

- **Duty to defend.** Colorado applies a "complaint rule": the duty arises if the complaint alleges facts that might fall within coverage (*Hecla Mining Co. v. New Hampshire Ins. Co.*, 811 P.2d 1083 (Colo. 1991)) [K]. III.7.1 ("even if the allegations are groundless") and III.7.4 are consistent with it.
- **Hammer clause.** Soft hammer clauses (70% here) are generally enforced as written; I located no Colorado decision [U]. The insurer's common-law duty of good faith in settlement still applies. Colorado allows insureds to assign bad-faith claims after an insurer's failure to defend or settle (*Nunn v. Mid-Century Ins. Co.*, 244 P.3d 116 (Colo. 2010)) [K]. The hammer does not displace that.

### C8. Voluntary payments

*Stresscon* (see A1): Colorado enforces no-voluntary-payments clauses without prejudice [S]. See D-2.

### C9. Insured v. insured

No cyber-specific decision was located [U]. **Drafting gap:** the IV.2.6 carve-back covers "an **employee's** claim about their own personal information". Partners and owners of a CPA firm are "executives" and insureds, not employees. A partner whose own tax data is breached and who sues the firm (or its management company) may be excluded. See D-23.

### C10. Other 2025–2026 cyber coverage decisions

- ***CiCi Enterprises, LP v. HSB Specialty Ins. Co.*** (N.D. Tex., Judge Lindsay, **Feb. 23, 2026**). A ransomware sublimit endorsement ($250K) did **not** cap the insurer's liability under the cyber-extortion insuring agreement; losses were over $1.2M [S]. https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/cyber/court-blocks-hsbs-ransomware-sublimit-in-firstofitskind-cyber-ruling-567006.aspx ; https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/insurance-laws-and-products/1770926/court-rejects-insurers-attempt-to-cap-cyber-extortion-coverage-based-on-ransomware-sub-limit
  - **For Harborline:** the III.1.6 ransomware coinsurance says "20% of covered loss arising from ransomware" but does not list which coverages it applies to (C only, or also B, D, F, I?). State them.
- **BIPA exclusions.** *West Bend Mut. Ins. Co. v. Krishna Schaumburg Tan*, 2021 IL 125978, held that a "violation of statutes" (TCPA/CAN-SPAM) exclusion did not bar BIPA claims. *Citizens Ins. Co. of Am. v. Wynndalco Enters.*, 70 F.4th 987 (7th Cir. 2023), found a catch-all exclusion ambiguous [K]. **For Harborline:** naming BIPA expressly in IV.2.10 avoids the ambiguity those insurers lost on.
- **CIPA and pixel coverage.** Commentary shows disputes over "violation of statutes" and "wrongful collection" exclusions and over whether "privacy liability" covers interception. **No 2025–26 appellate coverage decision on a cyber form was located** [S] https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/insurance-coverage/pixel-and-other-privacy-related-litigation/ ; https://www.dataprivacyandsecurityinsider.com/2025/09/navigating-cipa-lawsuits-loopholes-in-cyber-and-cgl-insurance-policies/
  - **For Harborline:** the separate *wrongful collection* definition and the option to buy it back (Q) are the clearest available structure.
- **War / hostile acts / widespread events.** No 2025–26 decision was located beyond the Merck settlement.

## Part E. Corrections to flag in the package, round 1 and the brief

1. **Brief:** "Principle Solutions v. ACE" should read ***Principle Solutions Group v. Ironshore Indemnity***, 944 F.3d 886 (11th Cir. 2019) [K].
2. **Round 1 on application 7.5:** "above CPA's 25,000 prong" is incomplete. That prong requires revenue or a discount from the sale of personal data, and the CPA has an entity-level GLBA exemption (no Colorado narrowing located). 7.5 "No" stands; fix only its explanation.
3. **Rationale "Conditions" row on late notice ("reduced only if the delay caused real harm"):** under *Craft*, Colorado would not require this for the claims-made outer deadline. Harborline is giving more than the law requires, and the wording does it by accident rather than design (D-1).
4. **Rationale "Still needs counsel or actuarial review":** it can now be specific. The fraud-cancellation notice must be **45 days** in Colorado. "Statutory interest" should read **8% compounded annually (C.R.S. 5-12-102)**. The **consent/voluntary-payments clause is enforced without prejudice (*Stresscon*)**. **Arbitration is not barred** for this line (10-3-1116(3) covers health, life and disability only). **Punitive damages are not insurable in Colorado (*Lira*)**.
5. **Rationale "Regulatory status" row on SB 690:** add "AG-only for website and app pen-register/trap-and-trace claims; retroactive to pending suits filed within two years before Jan. 1, 2027; §631 wiretap claims unaffected". Still awaiting the Governor (deadline Sept. 30) as of the latest snippets.
6. **Rationale "Exclusions" and "Definitions" row ("Punitive damages covered where insurable under the most favorable law … broadest lawful protection"):** for a Colorado insured under Colorado law this is likely illusory. Say so in plain English (D-9).
7. **TRIA:** Notice 7 lacks the required 80% federal share and $100B cap content, and the Item 12 cross-reference is circular (D-15).
