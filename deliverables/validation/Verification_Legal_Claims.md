## 1. Summary

**Counts (53 claims):** 37 Verified · 13 Partly · 2 Unverifiable · 1 Wrong.

**Top fixes, in priority order**
1. **California SB 690 description is wrong (R L292).** The final bill ends private suits under California's **pen-register and trap-and-trace** provision (Penal Code 638.51) for website and app tracking. It does **not** limit wiretap (section 631) suits. The status ("awaiting the Governor, deadline Sept 30, 2026") is still correct as of the latest sources.
2. **Colorado cancellation grounds (P V.5.2, V.3.2; R L244).** C.R.S. 10-4-109.7 has a closed list of valid reasons: non-payment; a false statement **knowingly made on the application**; or a substantial change in the risk from what the application showed. "Fraud" is broader than that list. The 10-day non-payment notice must state the reason. Part 3.2's 60-day cancellation right also contradicts V.5.2's "only".
3. **Renewal-change notice is missing required content (P V.5.3).** C.R.S. 10-4-110.5 requires notice by first-class mail at least 45 days ahead, giving the **reasons, the renewal terms and the premium due**. If the insurer misses this, the policy is deemed renewed on the same terms.
4. **Colorado fraud warning (P notice 8; A L191).** All the words match C.R.S. 10-1-128(6)(a), but one comma is missing: the statute reads "denial of insurance**,** and civil damages".
5. **War-exclusion rationale overstates novelty (R L164–168).** Lloyd's Y5381 *requires* an attribution basis. LMA 5567 already has attribution, the insurer's burden of proof and "State means sovereign state". Beazley's clause lacks attribution but already uses "sovereign state". Only the continued help while attribution is pending is a true addition.
6. **Case descriptions.** *Apache* turned on "resulting directly from the use of any computer", not "direct loss". *Travelers v. ICS* was a **stipulated** rescission with no merits ruling.
7. **Damages definition (P L348).** Colorado bars insuring punitive and exemplary damages (*Lira*). The rule for "multiplied" damages is not settled. Naming Colorado inside the one-size wording also breaks the "only the Declarations change" design.
8. **Application.** 7.5 misstates the Colorado Privacy Act's second threshold. 7.1 and 7.4 ask legal conclusions, which contradicts R L199.

**Re-check on submission day:** the CIRCIA Federal Register status; the Governor's action on SB 690 (deadline Sept 30); the CiCi docket number and any appeal; the Beazley form number E15626; TRIA reauthorization (S.4395).

## 3. Required notice text

### Colorado fraud warning: C.R.S. 10-1-128(6)(a)

The correct text follows. The only difference from both documents is the comma after "denial of insurance". The statute lowercases the agency names; capitalizing them is acceptable, but the text below follows the statute exactly.

> It is unlawful to knowingly provide false, incomplete, or misleading facts or information to an insurance company for the purpose of defrauding or attempting to defraud the company. Penalties may include imprisonment, fines, denial of insurance, and civil damages. Any insurance company or agent of an insurance company who knowingly provides false, incomplete, or misleading facts or information to a policyholder or claimant for the purpose of defrauding or attempting to defraud the policyholder or claimant with regard to a settlement or award payable from insurance proceeds shall be reported to the Colorado division of insurance within the department of regulatory agencies.

Where it goes: printed applications, policies and claim forms (per the search summary of 10-1-128(6)(a), level S). The package has it on the policy and the application. The **claim form** (not in the package) must carry it too.

Sources (S): https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-10/general-provisions/article-1/part-1/section-10-1-128/ ; https://insurancefraud.org/regulations/colorado-fraud-warning-section-10-1-128-6a/

Notice 7 is substantively compliant; no wording change is required. If a closer match to common model wording is wanted, this is a drop-in replacement (model-based, level K):

> Coverage for acts of terrorism certified under the federal Terrorism Risk Insurance Act, as amended, is included in this policy on the same terms, conditions and exclusions as other covered losses. Losses from certified acts of terrorism may be partly reimbursed by the United States Government under a formula set by federal law. Under that formula, the United States Government generally reimburses 80% of covered terrorism losses above the deductible that the law sets for the insurance company providing the coverage. The Act contains a $100 billion cap that limits U.S. Government reimbursement, and insurers' liability, for losses from certified acts of terrorism when those losses exceed $100 billion in any one calendar year. If the combined insured losses of all insurers exceed $100 billion, your coverage may be reduced. The U.S. Treasury confirmed on December 27, 2016 that stand-alone cyber policies fall within the program. The part of your annual premium for this coverage is $0.

Context (S): the program runs to December 31, 2027. The policy period ends October 15, 2027, so it falls within the program. S.4395 (a 7-year extension) is pending.
